### Definition and main characteristics

Sporting tourism is an active form of recreation combining sport, travel and learning about the world. It involves overcoming natural obstacles, using specialized equipment and having an appropriate level of physical fitness.

Its main characteristics are:

- Physical activity and exertion
- Overcoming natural obstacles
- Specialized equipment
- An element of competition
- Independence and self-sufficiency

### Differences from ordinary tourism

| Criterion | Sporting tourism | Ordinary tourism |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Completing a route and personal development | Rest and entertainment |
| Physical exertion | High | Low or moderate |
| Equipment | Specialized | Ordinary |
| Route | Demanding, often unfamiliar | Straightforward and familiar |
| Comfort | Minimal | High |

### Types of sporting tourism

Sporting tourism includes several disciplines, each requiring specific skills and preparation:

1. Walking
2. Mountain
3. Ski
4. Water
5. Cycling
6. Caving
7. Motor
8. Sailing

For example, mountain tourism requires skills with mountaineering equipment, while water tourism requires handling watercraft.

### Classification of walking expeditions

Walking expeditions in sporting tourism are divided into difficulty categories. This helps participants select a route suited to their preparation and experience.

#### Difficulty categories

| Category | Duration | Distance | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 6–8 days | 100–120 km | Simple routes, minimal obstacles |
| II | 8–10 days | 120–140 km | Moderate difficulty, easy passes |
| III | 10–13 days | 140–170 km | Difficult sections and high mountain passes |
| IV | 13–16 days | 170–210 km | Technically demanding routes and harsh conditions |
| V | 16–20 days | 210–240 km | Extreme routes and high self-sufficiency |
| VI | 20+ days | 240+ km | Maximum difficulty and distinctive routes |

#### Factors affecting the difficulty category

- Terrain
- Climate
- Natural obstacles
- Self-sufficiency required
- Technical difficulty

Choose a category according to participants' fitness, experience and skills. Beginners should start with category I–II expeditions and progress gradually. Higher categories require appropriate experience and specialized equipment.
