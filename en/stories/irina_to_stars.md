# The story of irina.to.stars: 4000 km on the Pacific Crest Trail (USA)

## Foreword

After placing "Wild" at the top of our film ranking, could we have imagined that a few months later we would meet a Russian woman who could have inspired its main character? Of course, the film already had a real-life prototype in the United States.

![](../../images/stories/irina.to.stars.png)

We met entirely by chance around a shared campfire at a hiking gathering. As everyone took turns telling their stories, one woman quietly said, "I walked four thousand kilometres of the [Pacific Crest Trail (PCT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Crest_Trail)." Her story, currently in diary form, can be read on Instagram: [https://www.instagram.com/irina.to.stars/](https://www.instagram.com/irina.to.stars/). We were especially interested in her initial motivation: what life problems had prompted such a demanding journey, and had the trip become a turning point that gave her the strength to resolve them? Her life story and reflections are grouped under these tags: [https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/pctmystory/](https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/pctmystory/) [https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/pctmythinking/](https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/pctmythinking/).

![](../../images/stories/irina.to.stars.instagram.png)

Readers can draw their own conclusions; here we will share ours.

Modern industrial civilization often drives people into a dead end, depriving their lives of meaning — an "existential vacuum" in the terminology of psychologist and philosopher [V. Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl). Remaining in this state for a long time is destructive. There are two ways out: a step down or a step up, primarily in outlook rather than intellect.

In the first case, a person suppresses the desire to understand who they are and lives by a primitive, robotic pattern that might be called "home–work". They remain human and contribute to civilization, but does this contribution match their strengths and abilities? This approach also puts an end to inner development.

The second way is a step upward: enriching life with meaning suited to the strengths and abilities nature has given us and the knowledge gained from books and the many hours spent in school and university classrooms.

To take this "step up", many people, though not everyone, need a tool to break out of the cycle of everyday life. Hiking, mountain tourism, mountaineering and similar activities can provide such a tool, because they involve overcoming personal limitations and external difficulties, and strengthening character.

This tool can help many people. Some may not need to walk 4,000 km: 400 km or even 40 km may be enough of a shake-up. Four kilometres will probably not be enough, though they can still help maintain your energy afterward.

Hiking offers a chance to be alone and reflect on life when traveling solo, or to talk with people who may share your motivations when traveling in a group. Both experiences can be combined. Irina's diary is an example: although she walked the PCT alone for six months, she constantly met people and shared some of their life stories on Instagram.

Below are passages from Irina's story that seemed central to understanding it. Of course, we do not claim to have understood it completely :).

## Before the PCT: biography

* I grew up in an ordinary Soviet family, with Soviet attitudes and a shortage of money... My parents gave me the best education: free mathematics education... As a child I was a timid, downtrodden girl, and the road towards awareness was long and difficult... I already have a grown-up child, the best daughter in the world. I had a promising office job, a roof over my head, family and comfort.
* I had worked in an office for four and a half years... I had dreamed of that job during a long maternity leave... It was my first office job... I climbed the career ladder, earned a good salary and handled interesting, complex projects... For many people it would be a dream... But I did not enjoy the work. Something was wrong. I could not understand what, but knew something had to change...
* At home, a relationship that had lasted fourteen years... A long time, but increasingly I realized how different we were in our outlook, goals, desires and dreams... Deciding to leave was hard: family is the most sacred thing to me.
* We have only one life, and I finally made a crazy plan: leave work, fill those eight hours with something enjoyable and truly mine, rent an apartment, move out from my husband with my daughter, then return to the office... Five months for everything... But something went wrong. I quit, moved into my own place, even if rented, and could not return to office work... Not for me? Then what is for me?

## What I worked on during the PCT

* Finishing what I start: a clearly defined goal leads to a result.
* Understanding my own value.
* Learning which goals are realistic right now.
* Setting boundaries and recognizing and protecting myself against dependence and manipulation.
* Changing my attitude to situations and dependence: who surrounds me, what do they mean to me, and how do they affect me?
* Trying to write a book to get feedback and avoid carrying mistakes into the printed version.
* Simply writing: learning to put thoughts into words and convey them to others as they exist in my head.
* Collecting stories about why people walk the PCT.

## PCT

* Yesterday I walked without water again, only nine kilometres but in the worst heat, with no wind or shade. I began hallucinating: a tree looked like an evil, grinning human face, and bushes like people... In the last kilometre my mouth was dry and I could barely move my legs, constantly muttering, "Water, water..."
* It took a month and a half to learn to move in the heat, adapt to the exertion and begin walking at least thirty kilometres every day... And to realize that I no longer want to wander in the desert, or at least not in summer and not for long... Ahead are my beloved mountains, probably the best part of the trail for me...
* Watching hikers: some listen to music through headphones, some play it loudly, some sing to themselves... I prefer listening to nature...
* Today I remembered that people's true attitude towards you appears in difficult times. When everything is fine, they want to talk and play with you. But when trouble comes and you need help, where are all those acquaintances? They disappear and throw the memories away... Games! I hate it...
* Watching others, I started noticing the same behavior in myself... You say one thing but act quite differently. I began to understand why people do not treat me as I would like...
* A discovery: I learned to feel joy and accept care from others, thanks to trail angels. I used to be an independent woman: never take anything, do everything myself. That "all by myself"! But when a trail angel offers sincere kindness, asking nothing in return, you smile and accept the gift joyfully and with an open heart. Sometimes I recognize my old problems in hikers who walk past and refuse gifts... Not all trail angels are sincere: some expect something, usually money. But Bill offers unlimited fruit and water every day. Could that be fake? Or the woman who drove me to town without asking for fuel money? This is genuine care from the heart. You can feel the attitude, and that is enough...

## What I gained on the PCT

* The ability to see the world more broadly: possibilities have no limits.
* We are all different, but we can all learn. Everyone has their own abilities and pace; these are characteristics, not barriers. Work, steps, aspirations, actions, luck, chance and time make the difference.
* Time is the most valuable resource; money cannot buy it.
* The past cannot be changed or erased. You can only accept it, in yourself and in others, understand it and move on.
* No single situation or experience can predict your future. Actions, thoughts and feelings belong to individuals and can take any form. They need not follow common sense, logic or statistics. You cannot change another person; you can only accept them.
* Nothing in life is guaranteed. What is happening now is already happening, and we shape the future ourselves, also now.
* Live in the moment: here and now!
* You are free to choose, and only you can decide what choice to make.
* Our nature is not dangerous. It is given to help us. Its beauty is boundless, and only we decide how far to open our eyes.
* Nothing happens for no reason; everything is given for something. Everything in life has a price. Rely on yourself, take responsibility for what happens and keep moving...

I am not perfect, but I am alive and see value in that.

## The Caucasus Trail: walking through unexplored Dagestan

"Returning in 2019 from my six-million-step walk across the United States, I wondered why Russia had no long-distance trails like the Camino de Santiago, Pacific Crest Trail, Appalachian Trail, Israel National Trail or even the Lycian Way. Searching for an answer introduced me to Dima Lemeshev, founder of Caucasus Explorer, who also dreamed of a walking trail in the Caucasus."

At the end of 2020, a joint project emerged to create a long-distance walking trail in Russia: around 2,000 km from the Black Sea to the Caspian Sea. Some sections will be easy walks with mountain guesthouse stays and opportunities to experience Caucasian culture. Others will cross wild, almost uninhabited highlands and gorges thick with subalpine vegetation.

The first exploratory expedition in Dagestan walked from Derbent to Kubachi along unfamiliar trails absent from maps. Topics include:

- Dagestani hospitality, and how the expedition became a food tour.
- Why not all snakes are dangerous and some "good" snakes eat "dangerous" ones, and how we entered a snake den.
- Jackals, foxes and bears: the story of our first sleepless night.
- Soviet-era paths that still exist but are difficult to follow, and constant struggles through thorny vegetation.
- Mountain beauty unfolding day by day: Alpine meadows and abandoned villages.
- Where to stay overnight and collect water.
- Where and how to eat.
- How to stay on a route that does not appear on maps.
- Communication coverage along the trail.

Speakers:
