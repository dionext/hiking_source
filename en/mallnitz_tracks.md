# Hiking around Mallnitz

This time we chose the small mountain village of Mallnitz (population 951) [http://www.mallnitz.at/en](http://www.mallnitz.at/en), in the far north of the Austrian province of Carinthia [http://www.kaernten.at](http://www.kaernten.at), as our base. Carinthia is best known for its relatively warm lakes; much of it has lower mountains, so visitors often come for swimming, relaxation and cycling, staying beside a large lake in the central part of the province. Our goal was different: mountain hiking. The highest mountains are in the north, beginning with the range along the Grossglockner High Alpine Road [https://www.grossglockner.at/gg/en/index](https://www.grossglockner.at/gg/en/index), then, from left to right, the Mölltal glacier, the Mallnitz valley, and the mountain road near Gmünd and Malta. Northern mountainous Carinthia, along the Salzburg border, belongs to Hohe Tauern National Park: https://venagid.ru/8227-vysokij-tauern-nacionalnyj-park-v-avstrii . Carinthia's central location is another advantage: Ljubljana is about an hour away by car and Venice about three hours. I arrived by the latter route in reverse, starting in the seaside resort of Lido di Jesolo. Buses run from there to Venice every half hour; the journey takes about an hour and costs around five euros. I then took an Austrian Railways bus: 3.5 hours, with a discounted fare of 19 euros. It runs a couple of times a day to Villach and Klagenfurt, from where you continue to Mallnitz by train. From Vienna there are direct trains or straightforward connections, taking about five hours.

A digression: people often look surprised when they hear that reaching the destination from the airport takes five or six hours. Why make things complicated instead of choosing somewhere closer? In my view, that is not necessarily easier. Alpine mountain areas are generally some distance from major airports such as Vienna, Munich, Verona or Milan. We did not consider smaller airports, including Klagenfurt, because there were no direct flights from our departure city. A train journey of at least a couple of hours was therefore inevitable. Five hours on a comfortable train is not much more tiring than two, and lets you get much deeper into the Alps, which is always more interesting.

For me, Mallnitz's railway station is a major advantage. With all respect to Austrian buses, I trust train timetables more, and trains also run later. You can easily spend an interesting week in the mountains around Mallnitz without traveling far. To explore all of Carinthia, renting a car is more convenient; rental offices are available in Villach and Klagenfurt. We did not rent a car, so this trip was limited to Mallnitz, Bad Gastein (ten minutes by train) and Flattach (forty minutes by bus).

The Alpe-Adria-Trail ([http://alpe-adria-trail.com/en/](http://alpe-adria-trail.com/en/)) passes through Carinthia: a varied, 750 km route through Austria, Italy and Slovenia, and three cultures. Walking its entire length takes much more than a day or even a week. Most people walk short sections, and tour operators arrange overnight stays in mountain huts. Even short organized tours can be expensive, so for now I prefer a less romantic arrangement that gives more freedom for the same money: reasonably comfortable accommodation at one mountain base and daily out-and-back hikes in different directions, occasionally using buses. We included part of the Alpe-Adria-Trail in these day hikes.

Unless you are a Rockefeller, it is worth asking about discount schemes when visiting an Alpine region. They vary considerably: some cards are given to all hotel guests, others must be purchased at tourist offices; some include free transport, others do not; some offer substantial cable car savings, others only small discounts. Schemes may change each year. In 2018, Carinthia had the Kärnten Card: [https://www.kaerntencard.at/en/](https://www.kaerntencard.at/en/). It cost 44 euros for a week and could be purchased at regional tourist offices, apparently also online. We received ours free with our accommodation. It offered 50% off regional bus and train travel, free use of key cable cars and other discounts detailed on the website. It generally paid for itself: a single return cable car ride from Mallnitz cost 27 euros, or 19.50 euros for the ascent alone. The card applies only in Carinthia. Bad Gastein is ten minutes away but in another province, so it has a separate card, available only to local hotel guests rather than for purchase.

Bad Gastein, a spa town and ski resort ([https://venagid.ru/8120-bad-gastein](https://venagid.ru/8120-bad-gastein)), is also a good summer base, though it is larger and more upscale than Mallnitz.

Mallnitz itself is small. The railway station is on the outskirts, about a fifteen-minute walk from the center. An hourly bus is useful for reaching the lower cable car station, three or four kilometres from the center. Walking there is also pleasant, with dedicated trails allowing you to avoid the road. Traffic beyond the station is very light. Mallnitz is effectively in a dead-end valley: to the north, towards Bad Gastein, there is only a railway tunnel. There is no road tunnel, but an hourly vehicle shuttle train carries cars for 17 euros.

Mallnitz has one large grocery supermarket, open every day and until 19:00 on weekdays, plus a few bakeries and cafés. As a winter ski resort, it also has sports shops. The town has a swimming pool and sauna: admission was six euros for the pool and eighteen for the sauna. Overall, it is quiet and pleasant.

Most walking routes are mountain hikes. The main ones begin at the middle and upper cable car stations, while other routes follow several small valleys from the village.

Our itinerary was as follows.

Day 1. We arrived around lunchtime, checked into our apartment and explored the village.

Day 2. From the upper cable car station [www.ankogel-ski.at](http://www.ankogel-ski.at) towards Ankogel summit. The route was demanding, partly over snow, with dazzling sunshine and wind that took some getting used to. We turned back after about four-fifths of the ascent, then walked down to Mallnitz without using the cable car.

Day 3. A walk along the river valley. We followed a mountain trail to Jamnigalm and returned partly by road for variety. A bus runs there twice daily, morning and evening. An alternative is to take the morning bus to Jamnigalm, make a higher mountain ascent from there and return on the evening bus.

Day 4. A trip to Bad Gastein, where we were surprised to find an interesting walking route ([http://21vu.ru/aus/35356-kaiser-wilhelm-promanade-bad-gastein](http://21vu.ru/aus/35356-kaiser-wilhelm-promanade-bad-gastein)). It runs partly through the upper town, with beautiful valley views, and partly beside a river with spectacular waterfalls. The tourist office told us that composer Schubert and Kaiser Wilhelm enjoyed this walk.

Day 5. A trip to Flattach [http://www.flattach.at/en](http://www.flattach.at/en), reached from Mallnitz by bus with one change. We got off one stop before Flattach to explore the Ragga river gorge, then took the same bus a couple of stops to Obervellach. Another mountain gorge leads to a trail back to Mallnitz from its upper end. We arrived back late in the evening. Nearby is the Mölltal glacier and year-round ski area, where, according to the guidebook, Austria's ski team sometimes trains. Unfortunately, no scheduled buses serve its lower cable car station, even though that cable car is included in the Kärnten Card.

Day 6. The highlight of our hiking: crossing a 2,450 m mountain pass from the upper cable car station to Bad Gastein, or more precisely Böckstein. Drizzle made the descent considerably more difficult. We returned by train.

Day 7. The weather deteriorated completely, with persistent light rain. We made a relatively easy hike from the upper cable car station to the nearest 2,700 m summit, looked down through the clouds and descended.

One planned excursion we did not manage was the Kölnbreinsperre dam [https://www.verbund.com/de-at/ueber-verbund/besucherzentren/malta](https://www.verbund.com/de-at/ueber-verbund/besucherzentren/malta). There were no scheduled buses, although a special tourist bus from Gmünd was advertised from 4 July. Reaching Gmünd from Mallnitz is not simple either: first a train to Spittal, then a bus. The tourist bus required advance booking by phone, but nobody answered; perhaps its start date had been postponed. The region's main tourist season was only beginning around our visit in late June and early July. For example, the Mallnitz cable car was listed as starting on 24 July. Earlier travel therefore makes little sense if your goal is high mountain hiking, especially as much snow may remain.
